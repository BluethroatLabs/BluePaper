How BluePaper works

Questions

01What does BluePaper do?

BluePaper turns an untrusted document into a safe PDF. It opens the original only inside a disposable, network-isolated sandbox, renders every page to pixels, and rebuilds a new PDF from those pixels on the trusted side.

02What is removed from the safe PDF?

Anything that is not visible on the rendered page: JavaScript, macros, embedded files, attachments, form fields, clickable links, and most metadata. What you can see stays, including a suspicious link or QR code printed on the page.

03Does zero regexp hits mean the file is clean?

No. The report lists raw byte indicators in the original file. Compression and encodings can hide them, and a hit does not prove an active construct was present. Safety comes from the pixel rebuild, not from the report.

04Is BluePaper free and open source?

The web console is free to use after a human check. BluePaper is AGPL-3.0 software, a network-service fork of Dangerzone, and its corresponding source is on GitHub.
